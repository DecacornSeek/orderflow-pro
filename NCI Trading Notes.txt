
Functional Specification: The NCI Trading Strategy for Algorithmic Implementation

1. Executive Logic: The "Real Story" of Market Liquidity

The NCI (Nature, Context, Impact) strategy is a liquidity-tracking framework designed to model institutional order flow. For an algorithmic implementation in Python, the system must move beyond geometric pattern recognition to model "Turning Points"—specific coordinates where liquidity shifts from selling pressure to buying pressure (or vice versa). The algorithm tracks the "Liquidity Line" length; institutional buying pressure is mathematically represented as long liquidity expansion at discounted price levels.

The core conceptual framework distinguishes between "Pulse Waves" (high-volume institutional expansion in the direction of the Main Stream) and "Pullback Waves" (temporary corrective volatility). By following the "Main Stream" established by Market Makers and Central Banks, the AI filters out retail noise. This liquidity logic provides the underlying "why" for the structural rules, framing the market as a series of institutional expansion and re-accumulation phases.

2. Market Structure Architecture: Defining Trends and Key Levels

In NCI logic, trends are treated as strict hierarchical sequences. The algorithm’s primary objective is the identification of the "Key Level," which serves as the definitive turning point of institutional interest. A trend is only considered invalidated when price produces a Validated Breakout (see Section 4) that closes beyond the Key Level. Retail "touches" or "wicks" that do not meet validation standards are discarded as noise.

3. Candlestick Classification and Signal Pressure

The NCI system treats candlesticks as "pressure gauges." The algorithm must evaluate the ratio between the body and total length to determine momentum consistency.

* Marubozu (High Pressure): BodyHeight / TotalLength > 0.7. This represents consistent momentum and institutional control.
* Pinbar (High Fluctuation): BodyHeight / TotalLength < 0.5. This represents high-volatility struggle and indecision where buying and selling pressures neutralized each other.
* Doji (No Power): Very small candles where both volume and range are negligible. These should be filtered as NO_SIGNAL or used only as minor confirmations.

The Special Marubozu (The "Shock Move") This represents a rapid "Shock Move" where price absorbs all opposing pressure within a single session. For Python implementation, use the following Fibonacci calculation:

1. Body Ratio: BodyHeight / TotalLength >= 0.5.
2. Tail: Must possess a long tail opposite the close.
3. Fibonacci Close: (Close - Low) / (High - Low) for bullish moves or (High - Close) / (High - Low) for bearish moves. If the value is \ge 0.9, the candle is classified as a Special Marubozu.

4. Validation Algorithms: Pullbacks and Breakouts

Price movement is only "valid" if it satisfies strict momentum standards. These filters prevent the algorithm from engaging during "Fake Breakouts" or weak corrective moves.

Functional Logic for Valid Pullbacks A corrective move must meet one of two standards to be considered a valid pullback:

1. 2-Marubozu Standard: Two consecutive Marubozu candles in the opposite direction of the trend. Constraint: Total length of the second candle must be \ge 70\% of the first to ensure pressure consistency.
2. Big Maru + 1 Small: One "Big Maru" followed by a small candle. Constraint: "Big" is defined as a Marubozu with a total length greater than the max() of the last 5 Marubozus. The subsequent small candle must maintain a close above the 50% retracement level of the Big Maru.

Functional Logic for Valid Breakouts (The 30% Body Rule) For a breakout beyond a Key Level or Range Boundary to be valid, the breaking candle must satisfy: abs(Close - BreakoutLine) / abs(Open - Close) >= 0.3 (At least 30% of the candle's body must close beyond the level).

Price Action Confirmation (The 4-Candle Rule) If a breakout fails the 30% Body Rule, the system enters a PENDING_VALIDATION state for the next 4 bars. If any subsequent candle produces a close beyond the original breakout candle’s high (for uptrends) or low (for downtrends), the state transitions to VALID.

5. Range Detection: Filtering Market Noise

Range logic serves as a "Skip Trading" filter. A range is initiated by a "Mother Candle" when subsequent close prices remain within its High-Low boundaries for 2 to 5 consecutive periods.

* Range Initiation: Triggered by Marubozus, Pinbars, or Invalid Pullbacks.
* Boundary Management: The RangeHigh and RangeLow are initially the High and Low of the Mother Candle.
* Dynamic Range Expansion: If a "Fake Breakout" (a move that crosses the boundary but fails Section 4 validation) occurs, the algorithm must update the RangeBoundary to the extreme of that failed attempt. The system remains in SKIP mode until a Valid Breakout occurs beyond these dynamically expanded boundaries.

6. Multi-Timeframe Confluence and Execution

Trading a single timeframe is high-risk. The "Confluence Logic Gate" ensures execution only when the High Timeframe (HTF) bias and Local Timeframe (LTF) structure are synchronized.

1. HTF Trend: Price > HTF_KeyLevel (for Buy).
2. Mid-TF Trend: Price > Mid-TF_KeyLevel (for Buy).
3. LTF Alignment: If the LTF is in a pullback (Downtrend), the system waits for a Change of Character (CHoCH)—defined as a Valid Breakout of the LTF's Lower-High (LH)—to realign with the HTF bias.
