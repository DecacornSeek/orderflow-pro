Technical Requirements


The NCI Candle Signal Taxonomy
The NCI framework ignores traditional candlestick nomenclature in favor of a "pressure-first" taxonomy. We analyze the relationship between the candle body and total length to filter out low-probability setups. This ensures the system only reacts to signals demonstrating genuine institutional commitment.
Primary Candle Classifications
Marubozu (High Pressure):
Standard: The body must constitute at least 70% of the total candle length.
Strategic Role: This signifies absolute dominance and serves as the primary engine for pulse waves and breakout validation.
Special Marubozu (Shock Move):
Standard: The body must be at least 50% of the total length.
Mathematical Requirement: Using a Fibonacci retracement from low to high, the closing price must reside within the 0.9 to 1.0 (90%–100%) range.
Strategic Role: This is a "shock move" signifying extreme speed and volatility, where price absorbs all opposing pressure in a single window.
Pinbar and Doji (Indecision & No Power):
Pinbar (High Fluctuation): Defined by a body less than 50% of the total length. It represents a struggle between buyers and sellers where neither has gained control.
Doji (No Power): A small candle with negligible body and length. It indicates a total lack of institutional interest or participation.
The Role of Candle Color
In the NCI system, color is critical for Marubozu candles as it defines the direction of institutional pressure. Conversely, the color of Dojis and Pinbars is treated as noise and ignored; these signals represent structural indecision regardless of the closing color.
Individual candles only become actionable when they aggregate into valid market movements, specifically the pulse and the pullback.

3. Defining the Valid Pullback (POI vs. BRT)
Identifying "real" pullbacks is strategically necessary to avoid the trap of internal noise. A valid pullback must demonstrate counter-trend institutional pressure before the primary trend is cleared for continuation.
NCI Pullback Standards
Two Marubozu Standard: Requires two consecutive Marubozu candles (70% body rule) moving against the trend. To ensure consistent pressure, the total length of the second candle must be at least 70% of the first.
Big Maru + One Small (50% Retracement Rule): A single large Marubozu followed by a smaller candle. To validate this as a pullback rather than a reversal, the lowest low of the second candle (in an uptrend) must remain above the 50% mid-point of the first candle’s total length.
Liquidity Zones: BRT vs. POI
The strength of the pulse wave dictates which zone the market retests for liquidity:
Break and Retest (BRT): Occurs when the pulse wave is "super strong." Price only retraces to the recent high/low (the breakout line) before immediate continuation.
Point of Interest (POI): This is the Equilibrium Zone, calculated as the 50% midpoint between the Recent High and the Key Level. Smart money enters here when pulse waves have moderate power, requiring a deeper liquidity grab.
Validating the escape from these zones requires precise breakout standards to distinguish between institutional momentum and retail fakeouts.

4. Valid Breakout Standards and Fakeout Detection
Fake breakouts are the primary mechanism for retail capital extraction. NCI standards protect capital by requiring verified institutional momentum before a level is officially "broken."
Breakout Requirements
Two Marubozu Rule: Two consecutive Marubozu candles must both close beyond the Breakout Line. This confirms the shift in pressure is sustained and not a temporary spike.
Big Maru + Small (30% Body Rule): If the breakout is led by a single large candle, a minimum of 30% of that candle's body must reside beyond the breakout line. This ensures the "Big Boys" have successfully pushed price out of the previous zone.
Price Action Confirmation (PAC) Protocol
If a breakout fails the initial standards but price remains near the boundary, we activate the PAC "Next 4 Candles" Rule.
Validation: A breakout is validated if at least one normal candle closes above the original high (or below the original low) of the breakout pattern within a 4-candle window.
Condition: If no such close occurs, the move is classified as a fakeout, and the level is considered weakened.

5. Algorithmic Framework for PINE Script Development
To translate these discretionary rules into a systematic architecture, the developer must implement the following multi-step logic flow.
Step 1: Trend Identification and Key Level Anchoring
Boolean Check: Define trend based on HH/HL or LL/LH sequences.
Key Level Mapping: Set the KeyLevel_Coordinate at the specific HL/LH that preceded the current HH/LL extreme.
Step 2: Candle Classification Engine
Standard Marubozu: If (Body / TotalLength) >= 0.70 = TRUE.
Special Marubozu: If (Body / TotalLength) >= 0.50 AND (Close >= 0.90 * High + 0.10 * Low) = TRUE (for bullish).
Pinbar: If (Body / TotalLength) < 0.50 = TRUE.
Step 3: Pullback and Breakout Validation Logic
30% Offset Condition: If (Close - BreakoutLine) / (Close - Open) >= 0.30 = TRUE.
Relative Length Condition: If (Candle2_TotalLength / Candle1_TotalLength) >= 0.70 = TRUE.
Retracement Condition: If (Candle2_Low > (Candle1_High + Candle1_Low) / 2) = TRUE.
Step 4: Zone Mapping and Coordinate Calculation
Key Level: Set as primary stop-loss boundary.
BRT Zone: Coordinate mapped to the previous Recent High/Low.
POI Zone: POI_Coordinate = (RecentHigh + KeyLevel_HL) / 2 (Equilibrium).

6. Interpretation of Trend Quality: Setup Grading
Not all setups are created equal; trend context determines probability. The presence of ranging price action or "Normal" candles dictates the quality grade.
High-Quality Setup (Trending): Characteristics include series of Marubozu pulse waves and pullbacks that respect the Key Level or POI without sideways consolidation.
Medium-Quality Setup (Corrective): Includes "Normal" candles (neither Marubozu nor Pinbar). These setups require PAC before a breakout is validated.
Low-Quality Setup (Ranging): If price is stuck within a single candle’s range for 2–5 candles, the level is "weaker." Breakouts from ranges require mandatory PAC verification.
The Gap Rule: Critical Capital Protection
Gaps represent liquidity voids. For monitoring purposes, gaps are treated as Marubozu candles to maintain structural counts. However, execution must be explicitly skipped during gap-heavy environments due to slippage and liquidity risk. Traders must wait for price to stabilize and escape the gap-affected zone via a valid breakout before committing capital.
The ultimate edge in the NCI system is not the discovery of patterns, but the ruthless discipline required to remain sidelined until institutional momentum definitively escapes the noise through a verified breakout.
